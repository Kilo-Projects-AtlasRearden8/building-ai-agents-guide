# Planning: Breaking a Goal Into Steps

An agent without a plan is like a driver without a map — it might move, but probably not where you want it to go. Planning is the first and most important building block.

## What planning means for an agent

Planning means turning a big goal into a small, ordered list of steps the agent can work through one at a time. Each step should be concrete enough that the agent (or you) can tell when it's done.

## A simple example

Goal: "Plan a three-day trip to Chicago."

A good plan looks like this:

1. Pick travel dates and how to get there
2. Find a place to stay within budget
3. Choose two or three things to do each day
4. Draft a simple day-by-day schedule
5. Show the schedule to you for approval

Notice each step has a clear "done" signal. "Research Chicago" is too vague. "Choose two or three things to do" is specific.

## How to write a plan

Follow these steps, on paper or in a document:

1. **Write the goal in one sentence.** Example: "Draft a weekly newsletter for my book club."
2. **Ask: what needs to happen before this is finished?** List everything that comes to mind.
3. **Put the steps in order.** Some things must happen first (you can't send the newsletter before you write it).
4. **Mark steps that need your approval.** Anything that sends email, spends money, or changes someone's data gets a "you decide first" flag.
5. **Keep it short.** Five to eight steps is plenty. You can always add more later.

## Plans can change

Agents should be able to revise the plan if something goes wrong — a website is down, a tool gives a strange answer, or you change your mind. A plan is a suggestion, not a command.
